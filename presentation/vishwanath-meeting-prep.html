The 5 questions — ask these, then shut up

Ask about reality. Not hypotheticals.

"Would you use this?" gets polite lies. "What happened last time?" gets truth.

"In the last year — have you been burned by a candidate who wasn't who they claimed? A proxy, a fake profile, someone who couldn't do the job they interviewed for?"
Good signal: a specific story, a number, a name. Real pain.
No signal: "not really" / vague. This client may not be your buyer — note it and move on to the next one.
"Walk me through how you verify a candidate is real today. Who does it, how long does it take, what does it cost?"
Listen for: manual effort, "we just check LinkedIn," no process at all. The worse their current process, the bigger your opening.
"What happens if a fake or fraudulent hire gets through to one of your clients?"
Listen for: "we'd lose the client," "compliance nightmare," "security incident." The more serious the stated consequence, the higher they'll pay.
"If there was a 2-minute check that guaranteed the interviewee is the real, verified person — works globally — what would it be worth per candidate? Would you run a paid pilot on your next 50 hires?"
THIS IS THE ONE THAT MATTERS.
Good signal: a number, "yes to pilot," "send me pricing." → This is a buyer.
Soft: "depends" / "interesting idea" / "send me info." This is not a buyer yet.
"If you wanted this, who signs off on the purchase, and how does it need to plug into your hiring process?"
Listen for: the actual decision-maker and integration requirements. Shapes what gets built next.
Objections he will raise — your answers

Be ready for these. Don't get caught flat.

"Why would candidates agree to this? It adds friction."
It's 2 minutes on their phone — no app, no account. Candidates already do background checks. This is faster and less invasive than a police verification. And for a competitive role, they want to prove they're real — it's actually a differentiator for them.
"We already do background verification with [AuthBridge / OnGrid / etc.]"
Traditional BGV tells you who someone was — not whether the person on your video call is that person today. It doesn't run at the interview. It doesn't detect deepfakes or proxies. TrueHire is the layer that incumbents don't have.
"This sounds expensive / complicated to set up."
The recruiter sends a link. That's it. The candidate does everything on their phone. There's nothing to set up — we give you a login and you're live in minutes. First 500 verifications cost nothing. Pricing is per-candidate — no contract, no commitment.
"What about DPDP / data privacy?"
Candidates give explicit consent before the check starts. Didit is enterprise-grade (the same infrastructure banks use). Data minimization is built in — we don't store more than needed. This is actually better for compliance than an unverified manual process with no audit trail.
"What if a client's IT system doesn't allow links / needs integration?"
It's a URL. Works on any device, any browser, no integration needed for v1. If a client needs ATS integration, that's a Phase 2 feature — and we'll build it when they name their ATS.
Signals scorecard — after the 3 client calls

How to read what clients tell you

✓ Green light signals

  • Specific fraud story (a case, a number)
  • Gives a price range: "I'd pay ₹X"
  • Commits to a pilot: "yes, let's do 50"
  • Asks "when can we start?"
  • Wants to introduce you to their TA head
  • Mentions a specific budget or procurement process

✗ Not a signal — ignore

  • "Interesting idea!" (no commitment)
  • "Send me more information"
  • "Depends on pricing" (without giving a range)
  • "We'll think about it"
  • "Not really an issue for us" (wrong segment)
  • Enthusiastic but asks nothing specific

Score your 3 calls:

2+ green light signals → Build it. Full productize and launch. Greenlight
Strong pain but no money signal → Problem is real, buyer isn't urgent yet. Narrow target — who has it worst? Try BFSI/fintech clients. Refine
No real pain stories at all → Wrong segment. Pivot to clients hiring specifically for remote/global/Web3/IT roles. Pivot segment
